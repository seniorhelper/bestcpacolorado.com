Local knowledge matters in Colorado

Colorado's tax rules change at the city line. Sales tax rates, home-rule licensing and occupational privilege taxes can all differ a few miles apart. Here's what tends to come up in each community.

Colorado Springs

Military families, defense contractors and a booming service economy. Common needs: military-related tax rules, multi-state filings for people who move often, and payroll for growing local businesses.

Aurora

One of Colorado's largest and most diverse cities, home to the Anschutz Medical Campus. Aurora has its own occupational privilege tax and is a home-rule city for sales tax purposes.

Fort Collins

A university town with a thriving brewing, tech and research scene. Startups, side businesses and Colorado State University staff all bring different tax questions.

Boulder

Startups, researchers and outdoor brands. Equity compensation, angel investments and fast-growing companies mean tax planning matters early.

Lakewood

A large west-metro city full of established small businesses and families. Homeowners, landlords and trades businesses make up much of the demand.

Thornton

A fast-growing north-metro city with lots of young families and new businesses. Payroll setup and first-time business returns are common needs.

Arvada

Historic Olde Town plus growing neighborhoods. Retail, restaurants and home-based businesses lead the way.

Westminster

Between Denver and Boulder, with many commuters and contractors. Multi-city sales tax and home-office questions come up often.

Greeley

Agriculture, food processing and energy anchor the economy. Farm income, equipment depreciation and seasonal payroll all need specialized care.

Pueblo

Steel heritage, a growing arts district and lower living costs. Retirees, small manufacturers and family businesses are common clients.

Longmont

Tech, craft beverages and a strong small-business core. Many owners split time between Boulder County and the wider metro.

Loveland

Known for its sculpture and arts community, plus growing light industry. Artists and makers often need help with self-employment tax and sales tax on their work.

Castle Rock

One of the fastest-growing towns in Douglas County. New homeowners, contractors and franchise owners drive demand.

Broomfield

A city and county in one, with corporate campuses and commuters. Remote and hybrid workers often have multi-jurisdiction questions.

Grand Junction

The hub of the Western Slope: orchards, vineyards, energy and outdoor recreation. Agricultural and seasonal businesses benefit from year-round planning.

Denver?

Denver has its own occupational privilege tax, its own sales tax rules and a big small-business economy. We built a dedicated resource for it: Denver accountant help.

Questions people ask

Do I need a CPA in my own city?

Not necessarily. Most tax and bookkeeping work happens securely online. What matters more is a CPA who knows Colorado's state and local rules, including your city's sales tax setup.

Does my city collect its own sales tax?

Many Colorado home-rule cities do. Check the city's finance department or the state's SUTS portal to see whether your city participates.
